# Ironsight Armory

A browser firearms simulator built with Three.js (r128). Everything is procedural: weapon models, textures, soldiers, maps and audio.

- **518 real weapons** from 1888 to today across five eras, including 26 prototypes and experimental designs (G11, TKB-059, Gyrojet, SPIW, OICW…), with real calibres, rates of fire, capacities and muzzle velocities.
- **Psycho Arsenal**: a sixth, fictional era of 21 invented weapons, split into *Feasible* (twin-stack PDW, belt-fed shotgun, integrally suppressed .338, 8-bore breacher, duplex revolver, needle rifle, salvo rifle, smart SMG, gyrojet carbine, FRAG-12 auto-shotgun) and *Overkill* (handheld minigun with spin-up, charge-up coilgun, 7-barrel pepperbox, 20 mm revolver rifle, 30 mm grenade machine gun, twin .50, .50 BMG hand-cannon, seeker-rocket swarm pod, 14.5 mm thermite rifle, 4-bore double rifle). New mechanics: explosive rounds, seeker rounds, rotary spin-up, coil charge, heat-glowing shrouds. Unique attachments, ammo and finishes, two new factions and two new battle maps (Foundry Nine, Blacksite Omega).
- **Soldiers** with lathed anatomical bodies, faces, gloved or bare hands and per-era kit; a phase-based gait with strafing and backpedalling, kneeling, crouch-walking, low-ready, tactical sprint, reload hand paths, hit flinches and five staged death animations that drop the weapon.
- **Armory** with a live 3D showroom and thirteen attachment slots (optic, backup sight / magnifier, muzzle, barrel, underbarrel, lug/side rail, magazine, pistol grip, trigger & action, stock, sling, ammunition, finish) and about 330 real parts, gated by era, weapon and calibre: from the Pedersen Device, Maxim silencer and ZF39 to the Vortex XM157, ATACR 1–8×, EOTech G33, offset RMRs, SureFire SOCOM cans, M320 and M26 MASS, Geissele triggers, Magpul stocks, M855A1 and Mk 262 ammunition and shotgun chokes. Every part has a real weight and stat changes shown as chips; hovering one previews its effect on the stat bars, and the *Perks* panel lists what the build can do.
- **Interactive parts in the field**: variable-power scopes zoom with the **mouse wheel**; **H** flips a magnifier in or out, rolls the rifle onto a 45° offset dot or canted irons, uses a piggyback dot, or throws a SpecterDR's power lever; **O** folds or collapses the stock (handier, harder to control); **U** threads the suppressor off or on; **L** adds a strobe mode on strobe-capable lights that dazzles enemies in front of you; night-vision and thermal scopes show their own image when scoped; rangefinders and ballistic computers show range and the corrected hold; quick-change barrels cool on every reload; burst/auto sear groups add fire modes; less-lethal bean bags stun. **I** shows a gear check of every active weapon and kit perk.
- **Enemies customise their guns too**: every hostile rolls a build for its role, tier and time of day (marksmen get magnified optics and bipods, breachers short barrels, lights and chokes, gunners big magazines, night fighters IR lasers and night sights). Pick up their gun and you get their build.
- **Explosives**: 17 real types from WW1 to today — M67, Mk 2, F1, RGD-5, Mills bomb and M24 stick grenades; the RGO and RKG-3 impact grenades; M84 flash-bangs that blind hostiles; M18 smoke that blocks their line of sight; AN-M14 thermite and Molotov cocktails that burn; a dynamite bundle; TNT, C4 and satchel charges you place and detonate; and the M18A1 Claymore, which fires on hostiles in its front arc. **3** throws or places, **4** cycles. Pick up to four for a mission; the range has all of them. Hostiles throw grenades too, and you get a warning.
- **Firing range** out to 1,000 m with 26 target types: balloons, army pop-ups, shoot/no-shoot turners, a steel spinner, a pendulum swinger, a quadcopter drone, laminated glass, an exploding target, paper, IPSC, steel gongs, poppers, a mover, clay pigeons, plate racks, dueling trees, hostage targets, walking enemies, a barrier penetration test, 14 vehicles with mannequin crews (armour thickness, slope and calibre-based penetration), ballistic gel, a gel torso with skeleton, NIJ armour plates, bottles and era mannequins. Drag, gravity, crosswind and zeroing are simulated; the panel reports time of flight, impact velocity, energy, drop and group size.
- **Kit locker**: 239 items across ten slots (uniform, helmet, face, night vision, headset, armour carrier, plates, gloves, boots, pack) from armies worldwide, WW1 to today, with 24 presets, grouped as Military, **Bomb suits** (Med-Eng EOD-8, EOD 9, EOD 10E, SRS-5 search suit and helmets, Spider blast overboots) and **Medieval** (nasal helm, great helm, kettle hat, bascinet, armet, sallet, close helmet, morion, lobster-pot; gambeson, mail hauberk, brigandine, Milanese, Gothic and cuirassier harnesses; gauntlets, sabatons, heraldic surcoats). Bomb suits cut blast damage to a fraction and cover the limbs; plate stops blades, fragments and low-velocity pistol rounds but not rifles. Every item has perks drawn from what it really is: camouflage that suits or clashes with the map, loud hobnailed or quiet trail soles, glove grip, eye protection against flash-bangs, active hearing that marks nearby enemy movement around your sights, radios that intercept enemy contact calls, and pouches and packs that carry spare magazines and explosives. Every item is modelled to its real outline: helmet shells with their own cut lines, rails, shrouds, covers and chin straps; SAPI-cut plate bags, cummerbunds, collars and period-correct pouch layouts; NVGs hanging from the shroud; gas masks, headsets, lofted boots and packs with shoulder straps. Protection is computed per zone against real calibres, and the kit's weight slows you down.
- **Test your kit**: "Test it on a mannequin" puts a dummy wearing your exact kit on the range, facing front, rear, side or quarter. Shoot it to see which plates, soft armour, helmets and visors stop which rounds; stopped hits are marked grey and penetrations dark.
- **Lasers and night vision**: PEQ-15, PEQ-2, DBAL and laser grips project a working beam and dot that tighten hip fire. **L** cycles visible, IR, light and combined modes. **J** toggles NVG (monocular, binocular or quad tubes, green or white phosphor); IR lasers are only visible through NVG.
- **Solo missions** (the game's combat mode): clear terrorists from eight structures — a cargo ship with its quay, an oil rig with a second platform, a consulate, the Pier 9 warehouse, a night train, the Hotel Meridian, a hijacked airliner with its hangar and a walled desert compound — each set in a wide outlying district about 480 m across (the oil rig stays offshore) at dawn, day, dusk or night. Up to 27 hostiles in six roles (riflemen, machine gunners, marksmen, breachers, Juggernauts, a cell leader) wear real kit resolved by the same ballistic model as yours. They notice you gradually (distance, stance, movement, darkness, muzzle flash), hear footsteps and gunfire, call out to each other, search, patrol, duck behind cover between bursts, fight worse when wounded and throw grenades when you hide. The dead stay down and drop their weapons: **T** takes one or swaps it, **K** drops yours. Win by clearing the structure itself; the district's hostiles are optional. The Armory's *Test in a mission* button lets you pick the mission map, difficulty tier and time of day, then deploys with that gun.

- **Mass battlefield**: 12 v 12, 20 v 20 or 32 v 32 conquest on six 600–640 m battlefields (Shattered City, Dust Bowl, Iron Harbour, Airbase Kilo, Marshalling Yard, Black Forest) in any era — both armies carry that era's real arsenals and uniforms. Five capture points between two HQs; standing in a ring captures it (more soldiers, faster); every death costs a ticket and the side holding fewer points bleeds them. Bots attack points they don't hold, defend threatened ones, fight, throw grenades and respawn at their HQ or held points; you pick where to redeploy. Chaos: artillery barrages on contested points (listen for the whistle), mortar salvos from both sides, jets making bombing runs, burning wrecks and smoke. Friendlies wear blue chevrons; the minimap shows points, HQs, friendlies and spotted enemies.
- **Extraction**: a hardcore loot-and-extract mode. A persistent hideout with money, a stash, traders (every armory gun with the build you saved, every kit-locker item), an **auction house** and a raid log. Every few minutes a rare lot comes up for 3–7 minutes — top-rated helmets and plates, premium night vision, bomb suits and plate harnesses, fully kitted weapons with the most expensive parts on the market, prototypes, Psycho Arsenal guns and rare valuables. Rival bidders raise the price up to their own hidden limits; your bid is held until you are outbid, and a bid in the last 30 s adds 30 s. Raid eight original maps (Pinewood forest, Logistics Hub, Old Town, Meridian Plaza, Rail Junction, Northern Airfield, Desert Fortress, Portside Terminal), 800–880 m across (Portside 480 m), as a PMC with your own gear or as a scav with a free random loadout. Scavs guard and wander, rival PMCs roam and loot, and bosses with guards hold the central structures; scavs and PMCs fight each other too. Loot about 80 containers per map and every body with **F**, take weapons off the ground with **T**, manage your inventory and see the exfils and timer (**Tab**), bandage bleeding and heal (**5**) — health does not regenerate. Exfils are always open, open only in some raids, open late, cost money or need you to drop your backpack. Die and you lose what you brought (insurance may return it); extract and you keep everything.
- **Minimap**: **M** opens and closes it, **Shift+M** switches between the 180 m around you and the whole map. It shows every structure and piece of cover, your position and facing, the mission objective or the raid's main structure, and in raids every exfil (green open, amber conditional, red closed; off-screen ones are pinned to the edge with their distance).
- **Reloads built for each weapon**: every gun reloads the way it really does, with the support hand handling the actual objects. A fresh magazine comes out of the pouch while the empty one is dropped (modern speed reload) or stowed (tactical reloads and older eras). AK, FAL, M14 and SVD magazines rock in on their front lug. AR-15s get a slap on the bolt catch. H&K roller guns lock the cocking handle back first and finish with the HK slap. Right-side handles are worked over or under the gun. Pistols use the slide stop or an overhand slingshot, and the Luger has its toggle pulled. Mauser, Mosin, Springfield and Arisaka rifles take stripper clips thumbed down from the guides, with the empty clip kicked out by the bolt; Lee-Enfields take two chargers. Carcano, Steyr M95 and Berthier rifles load whole Mannlicher en-bloc clips (the empty one drops out of the bottom). The Garand's en-bloc is thumbed in and the bolt slams home, and on a partial reload the clip pings out. Shotguns, the Lebel and the Krag load round by round, and empty shotguns get a shell dropped into the ejection port first. Swing-out revolvers open, tip muzzle-up to dump the empties and reload from a speedloader (moon clips for the M1917, loose rounds for older guns); Nagant and Single Action revolvers load gate by gate, and top-breaks hinge open. Belt-fed guns open the cover, strip the old belt, swap the box, drum or pouch, lay the new belt and slam the cover. Lewis and DP pans are lifted off and seated, Bren and other top magazines hook in front-first, Sten and Sterling magazines go in from the side, and Thompson drums slide in sideways. The P90's top magazine, the G11's front-loaded block and crank, Bizon helical magazines, Jackhammer cassettes, Hotchkiss strips, the Breda 30's charger and psycho-arsenal power cells each have their own sequence. Small per-gun variations keep two guns of the same family from moving identically.
- **Weapon workshop**: design your own guns. *Merge*: tick two or more real guns from any era (in the workshop, or with the Armory's **Merge mode**) and get one weapon with each donor's strongest trait — the hardest-hitting cartridge, the fastest rate of fire, the biggest magazine, the tightest group, the quickest reload, the softest recoil and every special mechanic (bipods, explosive and seeker rounds, rotary spin-up, coil charge, salvo barrels, integral suppressors) — on a body assembled from the donors' receiver, barrel, stock and magazine; a panel shows which gun gave what. *Build from scratch*: start from any gun (or hit **Chaos build**) and change everything with a live 3D preview and stat bars — receiver type and dimensions, bolt/slide/charging handle, grip, stock, barrel length and bore, muzzle, handguard, magazine body, calibre, capacity, sights, colours and finishes, every real-gun extra, action, fire modes, rate, burst rate, velocity, damage, pellets, dispersion, recoil, reload times, weight and special mechanics — plus twelve workshop-only parts no real gun has (radiator fins, gun shield, coolant tank, glowing coil array, spiked crown, under-barrel blade, carry loop, twin side drums, under-barrel mini cannon, receiver armour, gyro stabiliser, exposed spine), each with its own effect. *Attachments*: make your own optics, suppressors, magazines, ammunition, stocks and more from any real part's look with your own numbers; they fit every gun. Designs are saved in the browser, appear in a new **Workshop** era and in every mode's weapon picker (range, missions, battlefield, extraction traders and stash, handling bench), take every attachment that suits their class, and can be exported and imported as a code.
- **Handling bench**: put any gun (including your designs) on a workbench and work it by hand with the mouse: drag the magazine out, click the ammo box to load rounds into it (hold to keep loading, Shift-click for five), click the magazine to seat it, drag the charging handle, bolt, slide, pump or lever to chamber a round, set the safety (**F**) and selector (**B**), and click the trigger to fire (hold for full auto) — with muzzle flash, recoil, cases spinning onto the bench, lock-back on the last round, dry clicks on an empty or spent chamber, live rounds flying out if you cycle a loaded gun, revolver cylinders that swing out and dump their empties, belt-fed feed covers, tube-fed shotguns, stripper-clip loading and the Garand's en-bloc ping. A panel tracks the chamber, feed and action and tells you the next step.
- **Open in Chrome**: a button at the top of every menu. Inside claude.ai it opens the game's page in a new Chrome tab — nothing to download; if the host blocks new tabs, it shows the link to click or copy (and still offers a single-file copy as an option). Elsewhere it hands the page to Chrome directly. It hides itself when the game is already running full-window in Chrome.

Open `index.html` through any static web server (the page loads `js/*.js`). Press **F1** in game for controls.
