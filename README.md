# Ironsight Armory

> Also in this repo: **[Ironclad](grimdark/)**, a procedural grimdark sci-fi FPS — see `grimdark/README.md`.
> And **[Gridlock: Legends of Motorsport](racing/)**, a racing game with 18 disciplines, 566 cars and 270 tracks — see `racing/README.md`.
> And **[Steel Meridian](meridian/)**, a 3D future-war RTS with 608 units, 20 maps with live weather, tech trees, a shop, crates and a battle pass — see `meridian/README.md`.

A browser firearms simulator built with Three.js (r128). Everything is procedural: weapon models, textures, soldiers, maps and audio.

- **1,770 real weapons** from the 1400s to today across seven eras — two big batches added at least ten more to every era and class group where enough real designs exist (rifles, carbines, battle and assault rifles, marksman and sniper rifles, SMGs, machine guns, shotguns, sidearms, prototypes, muskets, cannons, mortars, anti-tank, anti-aircraft, field, siege and naval guns, recoilless rifles) — including 94 prototypes and experimental designs (G11, TKB-059, Gyrojet, SPIW, OICW, EM-2, Hughes Lockless, XM25, NGSW…), each with its own weight, recoil, spread and reload handling, with real calibres, rates of fire, capacities and muzzle velocities.
- **Black Powder** era: flintlocks, muskets and old cannons — the matchlock arquebus and Tanegashima, wheel-lock carbine and puffer pistol, Brown Bess, Charleville 1777, Potsdam and Springfield 1795 muskets, Baker, Kentucky and Jäger rifles, the Afghan jezail, blunderbusses and the Nock volley gun, the Girandoni air rifle, Pattern 1853 Enfield, Springfield 1861, Lorenz and Whitworth rifle-muskets, Sea Service, Wogdon duelling and An IX flintlock pistols, Colt Walker, 1851 Navy, 1860 Army, Remington 1858, LeMat and pepperbox cap-and-ball revolvers, Henry, Spencer and Winchester 1866/1873 lever guns, Dreyse and Chassepot needle guns, Martini–Henry, Snider, Trapdoor, Rolling Block, Sharps and Ferguson breech-loaders; and cannons from the falconet, swivel gun, minion and culverin to 6- and 12-pounder Napoleon field guns, Parrott, Ordnance and Armstrong rifles, naval long guns, carronades, the Dahlgren, Coehorn and 13-inch mortars, Mons Meg, the Great Turkish Bombard and the Tsar Cannon, plus the Puckle gun, the Korean hwacha, Congreve rockets and hand-cranked Gatlings. Locks have real lock time (the pan flashes before the shot), can flash in the pan, and every shot leaves a cloud of white smoke.
- **Artillery** era, from small to ultra big: Stokes, Brandt, 60/81/120 mm and 4.2-inch mortars up to the 240 mm Tyulpan, the 600 mm Karl-Gerät and the 914 mm Little David; anti-tank guns from the 37 mm Pak 36 and 2-pounder to the 17-pounder, Pak 40, Pak 43, ZiS-3 and MT-12; M40, B-10 and Carl Gustaf recoilless rifles; field guns and howitzers from the French 75, 18-pounder and 25-pounder through the leFH 18, M101, L118, D-30, ML-20, M114, Long Tom, M198, FH70, Msta-B and M777 to 203 mm howitzers; Bofors 40 mm, Flak 38, Flakvierling, Flak 36 88 mm, ZU-23-2, KS-19 and Skysweeper AA guns; super-heavies — Big Bertha, the Škoda 30.5 cm, the Paris Gun, K5 Leopold, the 18-inch railway howitzer, Schwerer Gustav and Atomic Annie; and naval guns from the OTO Melara 76 mm and 5-inch Mark 45 to the 15-inch, 16-inch/50 and 46 cm Yamato guns, plus Phalanx CIWS. Barrels run back on their recoil mechanism, mortars lob their bombs, recoilless rifles blow backblast, and emplaced guns turn slowly. Period ammunition (round shot, canister, grape, chain shot, heated shot, old shells, buckshot and Minié balls; HE, AP, APFSDS, flak, HEAT and incendiary mortar bombs) fits only the guns that fired it.
- **Double-barrel shotguns and more**: coach guns, sawn-offs and the lupara, side-by-sides (Purdey, Ithaca, AyA, Merkel, Webley & Scott) and over-unders (Beretta 686 and DT11, Browning Citori, Perazzi MX8, Krieghoff K-80, Blaser F16), a Sauer drilling, .470 and .600 Nitro Express double rifles, and single-shot break-actions (H&R Topper, Contender, M79), each breaking open on its own hinge and top lever.
- **Psycho Arsenal**: a sixth, fictional era of 62 invented weapons, split into *Feasible* (twin-stack PDW, belt-fed shotgun, integrally suppressed .338, 8-bore breacher, duplex revolver, needle rifle, salvo rifle, smart SMG, gyrojet carbine, FRAG-12 auto-shotgun) and *Overkill* (handheld minigun with spin-up, charge-up coilgun, 7-barrel pepperbox, 20 mm revolver rifle, 30 mm grenade machine gun, twin .50, .50 BMG hand-cannon, seeker-rocket swarm pod, 14.5 mm thermite rifle, 4-bore double rifle). New mechanics: explosive rounds, seeker rounds, rotary spin-up, coil charge, heat-glowing shrouds. Unique attachments, ammo and finishes, two new factions and two new battle maps (Foundry Nine, Blacksite Omega).
- **Soldiers** with lathed anatomical bodies, faces, gloved or bare hands and per-era kit; a phase-based gait with strafing and backpedalling, kneeling, crouch-walking, low-ready, tactical sprint, reload hand paths, hit flinches and five staged death animations that drop the weapon.
- **Classify the armory your way**: pick how the armory is grouped (*Weapon type*, *Era*, *Country*, *Calibre*, *Action*, *Decade*, *My groups* or *Everything*) and how each group is ordered (*Oldest first*, *Newest first*, *A – Z* or *Most used*, from your tracked record). Dividers follow the choice: periods, decades, years, initials or used / not used. *My groups* are your own named collections (a Favourites group to start with): add the gun on show to any group or a new one, take it out with the ✕ on the group's chip, and rename or delete groups. A gun can be in several groups. Your choices are remembered. By default the armory opens grouped by weapon type, oldest first, with prototypes in their class and the Psycho Arsenal and Workshop as their own groups. The bench, the workshop's merge picker, the extraction traders and the loadout dropdowns are grouped by type.
- **Emplaced guns stay on the ground**: cannons, field guns, howitzers, mortars, anti-tank, anti-aircraft, siege and naval guns, recoilless rifles and real guns too heavy to carry and fire (tripod machine guns, 20 mm anti-tank rifles) are no longer dragged around. While you man one you stand at its breech and can traverse, elevate and fire but not walk off with it. Switch weapons (1 / 2 / wheel) or press **K** and you step away: the gun stays where it is at full size, loaded as you left it. Walk back to its breech and press **T** to man it again.
- **Every gun has its own model**: no two of the 1,832 guns share a model any more. Each original hand-built model stays as it was. Every gun that used to be a copy of a relative (Mauser variants of the K98k, AK clones, Glock-pattern pistols, pump guns…) gets its own look, worked out from its id so it never changes: receiver and barrel proportions, magazine length and curve, muzzle device, sights, grip, stock, handguard length, wood, polymer colour and metal finish. Muskets and doubles also vary hammers, barrel layout, patchboxes, stocks and brass furniture, and cannon and artillery their wheels, shields and muzzle brakes. Every change comes from what real guns of the same action type and period use, and nothing that drives the mechanism is touched, so reloads, the bench and the inner workings still match.
- **Armory** with a live 3D showroom and thirteen attachment slots (optic, backup sight / magnifier, muzzle, barrel, underbarrel, lug/side rail, magazine, pistol grip, trigger & action, stock, sling, ammunition, finish) and about 330 real parts, gated by era, weapon and calibre: from the Pedersen Device, Maxim silencer and ZF39 to the Vortex XM157, ATACR 1–8×, EOTech G33, offset RMRs, SureFire SOCOM cans, M320 and M26 MASS, Geissele triggers, Magpul stocks, M855A1 and Mk 262 ammunition and shotgun chokes. Every part has a real weight and stat changes shown as chips; hovering one previews its effect on the stat bars, and the *Perks* panel lists what the build can do.
- **Interactive parts in the field**: variable-power scopes zoom with the **mouse wheel**; **H** flips a magnifier in or out, rolls the rifle onto a 45° offset dot or canted irons, uses a piggyback dot, or throws a SpecterDR's power lever; **O** folds or collapses the stock (handier, harder to control); **U** threads the suppressor off or on; **L** adds a strobe mode on strobe-capable lights that dazzles enemies in front of you; night-vision and thermal scopes show their own image when scoped; rangefinders and ballistic computers show range and the corrected hold; quick-change barrels cool on every reload; burst/auto sear groups add fire modes; less-lethal bean bags stun. **I** shows a gear check of every active weapon and kit perk.
- **Enemies customise their guns too**: every hostile rolls a build for its role, tier and time of day (marksmen get magnified optics and bipods, breachers short barrels, lights and chokes, gunners big magazines, night fighters IR lasers and night sights). Pick up their gun and you get their build.
- **Explosives**: 17 real types from WW1 to today — M67, Mk 2, F1, RGD-5, Mills bomb and M24 stick grenades; the RGO and RKG-3 impact grenades; M84 flash-bangs that blind hostiles; M18 smoke that blocks their line of sight; AN-M14 thermite and Molotov cocktails that burn; a dynamite bundle; TNT, C4 and satchel charges you place and detonate; and the M18A1 Claymore, which fires on hostiles in its front arc. **3** throws or places, **4** cycles. Pick up to four for a mission; the range has all of them. Hostiles throw grenades too, and you get a warning.
- **Firing range** out to 1,000 m with 26 target types: balloons, army pop-ups, shoot/no-shoot turners, a steel spinner, a pendulum swinger, a quadcopter drone, laminated glass, an exploding target, paper, IPSC, steel gongs, poppers, a mover, clay pigeons, plate racks, dueling trees, hostage targets, walking enemies, a barrier penetration test, 14 vehicles with mannequin crews (armour thickness, slope and calibre-based penetration), ballistic gel, a gel torso with skeleton, NIJ armour plates, bottles and era mannequins. Drag, gravity, crosswind and zeroing are simulated; the panel reports time of flight, impact velocity, energy, drop and group size.
- **Kit locker**: 435 items across ten slots (uniform, helmet, face, night vision, headset, armour carrier, plates, gloves, boots, pack) from armies worldwide, WW1 to today, with 24 presets, grouped as Military, **Bomb suits** (Med-Eng EOD-8, EOD 9, EOD 10E, SRS-5 search suit and helmets, Spider blast overboots) and **Medieval** (nasal helm, great helm, kettle hat, bascinet, armet, sallet, close helmet, morion, lobster-pot; gambeson, mail hauberk, brigandine, Milanese, Gothic and cuirassier harnesses; gauntlets, sabatons, heraldic surcoats). Bomb suits cut blast damage to a fraction and cover the limbs; plate stops blades, fragments and low-velocity pistol rounds but not rifles. Every item has perks drawn from what it really is: camouflage that suits or clashes with the map, loud hobnailed or quiet trail soles, glove grip, eye protection against flash-bangs, active hearing that marks nearby enemy movement around your sights, radios that intercept enemy contact calls, and pouches and packs that carry spare magazines and explosives. Every item is modelled to its real outline: helmet shells with their own cut lines, rails, shrouds, covers and chin straps; SAPI-cut plate bags, cummerbunds, collars and period-correct pouch layouts; NVGs hanging from the shroud; gas masks, headsets, lofted boots and packs with shoulder straps. Protection is computed per zone against real calibres, and the kit's weight slows you down.
  - **More kit**: 163 new items, including 43 uniforms (WW1 Italian, Austro-Hungarian, Ottoman and Russian, Denison smocks, Sumpftarn and oak leaf, French and Portuguese lizard, Strichtarn, Moro, Norwegian M75, AOR1/AOR2, PenCott, A-TACS AU, Estonian, Lithuanian, Hungarian, New Zealand and more, woodland and desert ghillie suits, arctic overwhites), 33 helmets and hats, 26 armour and load-bearing pieces (WW1 trench armour, Soviet 6B2/6B4, RBA, MTV, IPC, FCPC, JPC 2.0, PICO, Defender-2, Corsar, chest rigs, 37 and 58 pattern webbing), plus gas masks, night vision (including fused thermal), headsets, plates, gloves, boots and packs. A new **Police & SWAT** category covers patrol and tactical uniforms, riot and ballistic helmets, face shields, concealable vests, tactical vests, plate carriers, gloves, boots and a breacher pack. **64 ready-made kits** (40 new): the Somme, Verdun and Isonzo, Arnhem, the Pacific and the Winter War, Algeria, Rhodesia, MACV-SOG, the Falklands, Desert Storm, Mogadishu, Chechnya, Kosovo, Fallujah, scout snipers, arctic rangers, maritime boarding, modern Estonian, New Zealand, Ukrainian, Russian and PLA troops, CBRN recon, and police patrol, SWAT, riot police, GIGN and GSG 9. A saved kit using bomb-suit, medieval or new items now survives a reload.
- **Test your kit**: "Test it on a mannequin" puts a dummy wearing your exact kit on the range, facing front, rear, side or quarter. Shoot it to see which plates, soft armour, helmets and visors stop which rounds; stopped hits are marked grey and penetrations dark.
- **Lasers and night vision**: PEQ-15, PEQ-2, DBAL and laser grips project a working beam and dot that tighten hip fire. **L** cycles visible, IR, light and combined modes. **J** toggles NVG (monocular, binocular or quad tubes, green or white phosphor); IR lasers are only visible through NVG.
- **Solo missions** (the game's combat mode): clear terrorists from eight structures — a cargo ship with its quay, an oil rig with a second platform, a consulate, the Pier 9 warehouse, a night train, the Hotel Meridian, a hijacked airliner with its hangar and a walled desert compound — each set in a wide outlying district about 480 m across (the oil rig stays offshore) at dawn, day, dusk or night. Up to 27 hostiles in six roles (riflemen, machine gunners, marksmen, breachers, Juggernauts, a cell leader) wear real kit resolved by the same ballistic model as yours. They notice you gradually (distance, stance, movement, darkness, muzzle flash), hear footsteps and gunfire, call out to each other, search, patrol, duck behind cover between bursts, fight worse when wounded and throw grenades when you hide. The dead stay down and drop their weapons: **T** takes one or swaps it, **K** drops yours. Win by clearing the structure itself; the district's hostiles are optional. The Armory's *Test in a mission* button lets you pick the mission map, difficulty tier and time of day, then deploys with that gun.

- **Mass battlefield**: 12 v 12, 20 v 20 or 32 v 32 conquest on six 600–640 m battlefields (Shattered City, Dust Bowl, Iron Harbour, Airbase Kilo, Marshalling Yard, Black Forest) in any era — both armies carry that era's real arsenals and uniforms. Five capture points between two HQs; standing in a ring captures it (more soldiers, faster); every death costs a ticket and the side holding fewer points bleeds them. Bots attack points they don't hold, defend threatened ones, fight, throw grenades and respawn at their HQ or held points; you pick where to redeploy. Chaos: artillery barrages on contested points (listen for the whistle), mortar salvos from both sides, jets making bombing runs, burning wrecks and smoke. Friendlies wear blue chevrons; the minimap shows points, HQs, friendlies and spotted enemies.
- **Extraction**: a hardcore loot-and-extract mode. A persistent hideout with money, a stash, traders (every armory gun with the build you saved, every kit-locker item), an **auction house** and a raid log. Every few minutes a rare lot comes up for 3–7 minutes — top-rated helmets and plates, premium night vision, bomb suits and plate harnesses, fully kitted weapons with the most expensive parts on the market, prototypes, Psycho Arsenal guns and rare valuables. Rival bidders raise the price up to their own hidden limits; your bid is held until you are outbid, and a bid in the last 30 s adds 30 s. Raid eight original maps (Pinewood forest, Logistics Hub, Old Town, Meridian Plaza, Rail Junction, Northern Airfield, Desert Fortress, Portside Terminal), 800–880 m across (Portside 480 m), as a PMC with your own gear or as a scav with a free random loadout. Scavs guard and wander, rival PMCs roam and loot, and bosses with guards hold the central structures; scavs and PMCs fight each other too. Loot about 80 containers per map and every body with **F**, take weapons off the ground with **T**, manage your inventory and see the exfils and timer (**Tab**), bandage bleeding and heal (**5**) — health does not regenerate. Exfils are always open, open only in some raids, open late, cost money or need you to drop your backpack. Die and you lose what you brought (insurance may return it); extract and you keep everything.
- **Minimap**: **M** opens and closes it, **Shift+M** switches between the 180 m around you and the whole map. It shows every structure and piece of cover, your position and facing, the mission objective or the raid's main structure, and in raids every exfil (green open, amber conditional, red closed; off-screen ones are pinned to the edge with their distance).
- **Reloads built for each weapon**: every gun reloads the way it really does, with the support hand handling the actual objects. A fresh magazine comes out of the pouch while the empty one is dropped (modern speed reload) or stowed (tactical reloads and older eras). AK, FAL, M14 and SVD magazines rock in on their front lug. AR-15s get a slap on the bolt catch. H&K roller guns lock the cocking handle back first and finish with the HK slap. Right-side handles are worked over or under the gun. Pistols use the slide stop or an overhand slingshot, and the Luger has its toggle pulled. Mauser, Mosin, Springfield and Arisaka rifles take stripper clips thumbed down from the guides, with the empty clip kicked out by the bolt; Lee-Enfields take two chargers. Carcano, Steyr M95 and Berthier rifles load whole Mannlicher en-bloc clips (the empty one drops out of the bottom). The Garand's en-bloc is thumbed in and the bolt slams home, and on a partial reload the clip pings out. Shotguns, the Lebel and the Krag load round by round, and empty shotguns get a shell dropped into the ejection port first. Swing-out revolvers open, tip muzzle-up to dump the empties and reload from a speedloader (moon clips for the M1917, loose rounds for older guns); Nagant and Single Action revolvers load gate by gate, and top-breaks hinge open. Belt-fed guns (whose belted rounds point forward, tip toward the muzzle) open the cover, strip the old belt, swap the box, drum or pouch, lay the new belt and slam the cover. Lewis and DP pans are lifted off and seated, Bren and other top magazines hook in front-first, Sten and Sterling magazines go in from the side, and Thompson drums slide in sideways. The P90's top magazine, the G11's front-loaded block and crank, Bizon helical magazines, Jackhammer cassettes, Hotchkiss strips, the Breda 30's charger and psycho-arsenal power cells each have their own sequence. Muskets are loaded with their own ramrod, cannons are sponged and rammed by the crew, and doubles, falling blocks, cap-and-ball revolvers, mortars and breech-loading artillery each have their own drill. Small per-gun variations keep two guns of the same family from moving identically.
- **Weapon workshop**: design your own guns. *Merge*: tick two or more real guns from any era (in the workshop, or with the Armory's **Merge mode**) and get one weapon with each donor's strongest trait — the hardest-hitting cartridge, the fastest rate of fire, the biggest magazine, the tightest group, the quickest reload, the softest recoil and every special mechanic (bipods, explosive and seeker rounds, rotary spin-up, coil charge, salvo barrels, integral suppressors) — on a body assembled from the donors' receiver, barrel, stock and magazine; a panel shows which gun gave what. **Random merge** picks 2–10 guns, a random base and random edits (tick *include cannon & artillery* for the heavy stuff). *Build from scratch*: start from any gun (or hit **Chaos build**, **Randomize everything**, or the 🎲 on any section to roll just that part) and change everything with a live 3D preview and stat bars — receiver type and dimensions, bolt/slide/charging handle, grip, stock, barrel length and bore, muzzle, handguard, magazine body, calibre, capacity, sights, colours and finishes, every real-gun extra, action, fire modes, rate, burst rate, velocity, damage, pellets, dispersion, recoil, reload times, weight and special mechanics — plus twelve workshop-only parts no real gun has (radiator fins, gun shield, coolant tank, glowing coil array, spiked crown, under-barrel blade, carry loop, twin side drums, under-barrel mini cannon, receiver armour, gyro stabiliser, exposed spine), each with its own effect. *Attachments*: make your own optics, suppressors, magazines, ammunition, stocks and more from any real part's look with your own numbers; they fit every gun. Designs are saved in the browser, appear in a new **Workshop** era and in every mode's weapon picker (range, missions, battlefield, extraction traders and stash, handling bench), take every attachment that suits their class, and can be exported and imported as a code.
- **Handling bench**: put any gun (including your designs) on a workbench and work it by hand with the mouse: drag the magazine out, click the ammo box to load rounds into it (hold to keep loading, Shift-click for five), click the magazine to seat it, drag the charging handle, bolt, slide, pump or lever to chamber a round, set the safety (**F**) and selector (**B**), and click the trigger to fire (hold for full auto) — with muzzle flash, recoil, cases spinning onto the bench, lock-back on the last round, dry clicks on an empty or spent chamber, live rounds flying out if you cycle a loaded gun, revolver cylinders that swing out and dump their empties, belt-fed feed covers, tube-fed shotguns, stripper-clip loading and the Garand's en-bloc ping. A panel tracks the chamber, feed and action and tells you the next step. Every gun handles in its own way: shotguns and doubles break open and drop shells into the chambers; muskets are half-cocked, primed, loaded with a cartridge, rammed with their own ramrod and fully cocked; falling blocks drop on their lever; cap-and-ball revolvers are charged chamber by chamber and capped; cannons are sponged, loaded with a powder bag and shot, rammed, primed and fired with a linstock; breech-loading artillery is loaded and fired by lanyard; mortar bombs fire themselves when dropped; Gatlings are cranked and the hwacha's arrows lit.
  - **Take apart**: every gun field-strips step by step into its own parts (receiver, barrel, handguard, stock, grip, trigger group, magazine, action, sights, muzzle and extras), and inside each is its own operating system — about 35 of them, from the matchlock serpentine to Stoner direct impingement, AK long-stroke piston, HK roller delay, Browning short recoil, blowback, toggle locks, Garand and Mauser bolts, Gatling rotors and artillery sliding breech blocks — with working internal parts. Hover a part to name it and click to remove the next one, or reassemble in reverse order. **X-ray** shows the internals in place.
  - **Clean**: shooting fouls each gun (black powder very quickly). Push a bore brush and patches through from the right end of the bore and watch the patches come out cleaner, scrub the action, oil and wipe it down. In the field a dirty gun shoots wider and jams; black-powder guns flash in the pan more often.
  - **How it works**: almost every gun (1,468 of 1,485) has its real mechanism built inside it, sized from the gun: bolt carriers with gas keys or pistons, 2- to 7-lug rotating bolts and cam pins, rollers and locking pieces, tilting barrels and links, Luger and lever-action toggles, firing pins, strikers, extractors, ejectors, hammers, sears, disconnectors, auto sears, selectors, buffers, action springs that compress coil by coil, magazine followers, shell lifters and latches, revolver hands, cylinder stops and transfer bars, double-gun tumblers, cocking levers and underbolts, flintlock tumblers, V mainsprings and frizzen springs, falling blocks, artillery breech blocks, buffers and recuperators, mortar bombs with their charge increments, and Gatling cams and bolts — up to 25 moving parts per gun. Live ammunition goes through it all: the top round is stripped from the magazine (or lifted from the tube, or pushed out of the belt) and up the feed ramp, the bullet runs down the bore, gas flows to the action, and the case is extracted and kicked out of the port. Play the cycle at real speed or slowed down as far as 1/1000, scrub it with a slider, step phase by phase with real millisecond timings, and follow live read-outs (bolt travel, bolt rotation, spring compression, chamber pressure, bullet position and speed) and a pressure/travel chart. Switch between semi and auto, loop it, X-ray it, or take a close-up of the action. Click any internal part (in the list or in the 3D view) for what it does, what it is made of, its service life and how worn it is.
  - **Handling**: every gun is treated as a rigid body — each part and accessory with its own mass and position — giving its overall length, loaded weight, balance point, swing inertia, ADS time, free recoil (energy, velocity and momentum from the bullet and powder gas), muzzle rise, recoil recovery, typical trigger pull, lock time, cycle time and bolt speed, plus where the weight is. In the game these drive aim-down-sights time, sway, how far the gun lags behind your view when you turn and how fast it settles after a shot; the armory shows the same sheet.
  - **Wear and malfunctions**: every round fired (on the bench or in the field) wears the springs, extractor, firing pin and other parts against their service life; replace them on the bench. Fouling, a dry action and tired springs raise the stoppage rate. On the bench you get real stoppages and must clear them the right way: a stovepipe (rack it), failure to feed (tap the magazine, rack), double feed (lock back, strip the magazine, rack, reload), failure to extract (knock the case out with a rod), out of battery (push it home), a dud (wait, then rack it out) and hangfires that go off a second late. Any of them can be practised on demand.
- **The Quartermaster**: an in-house weapons instructor you can talk to (the **Quartermaster** button, bottom-right, on any menu screen). Ask about history, how mechanisms work, ballistics, cleaning, safe handling, the game's guns, builds, comparisons and what to try next. Opened inside claude.ai it is powered by Claude (the first message asks for your consent and uses your own Claude usage) and can look up and compare any gun in the armory, read your record and start lessons; opened anywhere else it answers from a built-in offline engine (gun facts, side-by-side comparisons, best-in-class lists, your record and advice, lessons). It will not help with making or illegally modifying guns, parts or ammunition.
  - **Animated lessons**: six lesson types for any gun — *How it works* (the firing cycle slowed right down in X-ray, part by part), *Field strip*, *Load and fire*, *Malfunction drills*, *Cleaning* and *Handling and recoil* — played on the handling bench with the gun's own model and animations, with pause, step back/forward and an “Ask about this” button.
  - **Your record**: everything is tracked — shots, hits, kills, headshots and time per gun, every range, mission, raid and battle session with its result, bench shots, malfunctions cleared, guns cleaned and lessons done — and turned into advice (accuracy against the class average, fouling and worn parts, guns worth trying, lessons you haven't done). Notes the Quartermaster saves about you are kept too.
- **Open in Chrome**: a button at the top of every menu. Inside claude.ai it opens a chooser with three ways out: the **direct game page** (recommended: the game's own hosted page with no claude.ai frame, so it runs full-window with real mouse capture and nothing to download; the Quartermaster uses its offline engine there), the **claude.ai page** (with the Claude-powered Quartermaster), and an **offline file** (one self-contained HTML file). Each link has *Open* (a Chrome intent on Android, the Chrome scheme on iOS) and *Copy link*. Outside claude.ai the button hands the page to Chrome directly, and it hides itself when the game is already running full-window in Chrome.

Open `index.html` through any static web server (the page loads `js/*.js`). Press **F1** in game for controls.
